Nanoparticle Indentation

Molecular dynamics simulations of high-strain-rate collisions of single-crystal aluminum nanoparticles — to find the optimal impact velocity for micro-cold spray coating applications.

Methodology

01

Setup

Defined FCC crystal lattice, 6 nm sphere diameter, and indenter geometry in LAMMPS input scripts.

02

Simulation

Ran high-strain-rate collisions across 9 impact velocities (1–1,000 m/s), collecting force and per-atom structure data.

03

Analysis

Used OVITO to classify atom structure (FCC, HCP, amorphous) and plotted force-depth curves across all velocities.

04

Publication

Findings contributed to a peer-reviewed paper on optimal impact conditions for micro-cold spray applications.

Challenges

  • Finding the velocity range that produces useful plastic deformation without full fragmentation
  • Learning crystal structure terminology — FCC, HCP, Burgers vectors, dislocation mechanics — with no prior background

Approach

Stayed curious and unafraid to ask questions. Continuously sought feedback, built domain knowledge iteratively, and used that understanding to tune simulation variables toward ideal deformation behavior.
