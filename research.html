Research & Publication

Molecular dynamics simulation research conducted as a Research Assistant — resulting in a peer-reviewed publication.

02 Research Assistant Completed

Nanoparticle Indentation

Conducted molecular dynamics simulations using LAMMPS to study high-strain-rate collisions of single-crystal aluminum nanoparticles. The goal was to determine the optimal impact velocity for applications such as micro-cold spray — a technique for coating surfaces without heat.

Published Paper
Simulation of High Strain Rate Contact of Single Crystal Al Spheres
Peer Reviewed

Simulation Code

Scripts controlled collision speed, crystal lattice orientation, indenter direction reversal, and data collection across thousands of timesteps.

Data Analysis & Visualization

Raw simulation output was processed into structural analysis and force-depth graphs. Crystal structure changes (FCC → HCP → amorphous) and impact force were plotted across a range of impact velocities from 1 m/s to 1,000 m/s.

Methodology

01

Setup

Defined nanoparticle parameters: FCC crystal lattice orientation, sphere diameter (6 nm), and indenter geometry in LAMMPS input scripts.

02

Simulation

Ran high-strain-rate collision simulations across 9 impact velocities. Collected force, displacement, and per-atom structural data at each timestep.

03

Analysis

Used OVITO to classify atom structure (FCC, HCP, other). Plotted force-depth curves and tracked dislocation nucleation and amorphization events.

04

Publication

Findings contributed to a peer-reviewed paper on optimal impact conditions for micro-cold spray applications.

Challenges

  • Determining which impact velocity range produced useful plastic deformation without full fragmentation
  • Learning crystal structure terminology — FCC, HCP, Burgers vectors, dislocation mechanics — with no prior background

Approach

Stayed intellectually curious and unafraid to ask questions, even "basic" ones. Continuously sought feedback, built domain knowledge iteratively, and used that understanding to tune simulation variables toward ideal deformation behavior.
