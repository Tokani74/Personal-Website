01 Personal Project Ongoing

AR Speech-to-Text Glasses

Designing glasses that display real-time subtitles on the lens — the device listens to nearby speech, sends audio via Bluetooth to a phone or laptop for STT processing, then receives and displays the transcribed text back on the lens.

KiCadOnshapePCB DesignBluetoothEmbedded Systems

What I Built

  • Custom CAD glasses frame with internal wire channels designed in Onshape
  • Two custom PCBs hand-assembled at the UT Austin Makerspace: a power board and a processing/display board
  • Complete KiCad schematics and PCB layout — full design completed in ~2 months
  • Modified an open-source smart glasses firmware model to fit specific hardware requirements

Obstacle

Unable to achieve consistent power delivery through the original monolithic PCB design.

Solution

Split the large PCB into multiple smaller, purpose-built boards for easier troubleshooting and probing. New prototypes have resolved several power issues — development is ongoing.

03 Personal Project Completed

Hand-Tracking RC Plane

A fully functional RC plane constructed from scratch and controlled entirely by hand gestures — a PC webcam tracks finger joints via Python and OpenCV, translating hand movement into servo commands transmitted by radio to an Arduino onboard the aircraft.

PythonOpenCVArduinoRadio TX/RX3D PrintingFoam Board

What I Built

  • Foam board airframe with hand-cut wings, fuselage, and control surfaces
  • 3D-printed propellers printed at UT Austin's Makerspace
  • Python/OpenCV hand-tracking: finger joint positions map to speed and direction inputs
  • Dual-Arduino radio system — ground transmitter parses Python output and sends packets to onboard receiver controlling servos

Obstacle

Finding the optimal motor power-to-propeller-size-to-aircraft-weight ratio through trial and error was inefficient and time-consuming.

Solution / Lesson Learned

While the plane ultimately flew, the key takeaway was to use aerodynamic lift calculations upfront rather than relying purely on physical iteration — saves time and material.

04 Team Competition Completed

Battlebot

A combat robot built to destroy opponents using rapid movement, a steel spinning weapon, and a hard 3D-printed PETG/TPU chassis — designed in SolidWorks and largely executed solo despite being a team project.

SolidWorks3D PrintingPETG / TPUESCLiPo SystemsRadio TX/RX

What I Built

  • Full SolidWorks CAD assembly: weapon system, PETG/TPU chassis, drivetrain, electronics mounting
  • 3D-printed chassis featuring ramped surface, structural trusses, fillets, and bearing seats
  • Electronics: LiPo battery, ESC, radio TX/RX, independent drive motors, weapon motor
  • Iterated on drivetrain design — next version uses a pulley rather than direct motor-to-hub drive

Obstacles

  • No prior battlebot experience on the team
  • Team conflicts left me as the sole contributor
  • Designing to strict weight and size constraints took far longer than expected

Solution

Create the prototype design much earlier in the timeline and build in explicit buffer for setbacks, revisions, and unexpected solo work.
